The Problem

Import Dependency

Bread consumption is high, yet Indonesia relies heavily on imported wheat flour to meet demand. A supply chain vulnerable to global price swings.

Health concerns

Standard wheat bread is low in fiber and carries a high glycemic index, and it's simply off-limits for anyone with gluten sensitivity.

Underused local commodities

Abundant local staples like banana and tempeh remain underused as ingredients for modern bakery products.

Our Solution

A loaf built on local, gluten-free ingredients.

We swap wheat flour for local alternative flours, held together without gluten.

Banana flour
Base substitute for wheat, locally abundant.
Xanthan gum
Binds the matrix in place of gluten.

Regular bread depends on gluten to trap gas and hold its shape. Without wheat, that job falls to xanthan gum — a binding agent that lets banana and tempeh flour rise and set like a proper loaf.

The result is a bread that's local by ingredient, gentler on the glycemic index, and safe for people avoiding gluten — without asking anyone to give up the everyday loaf.

STEM Approaches

Chemistry

Controls how the non-gluten network forms, and how the Maillard reaction builds the crust's color and flavor.

Biology

Optimizes how yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) breaks down sugar to produce the gas that makes dough rise.

Physics

Analyzes CO₂ expansion during baking (PV = nRT) and how heat transfers through the loaf.
